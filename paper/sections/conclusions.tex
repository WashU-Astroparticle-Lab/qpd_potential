We have built and executed an end-to-end, published-value-anchored forward
model of the reconstructed-energy background spectra for a thin single-sided
germanium QPD wafer. Starting from the deposited-energy spectra of the
reactor-CEvNS signal and of its surface backgrounds---through-going cosmic
muons, the radiogenic environmental-gamma continuum, the ambient fast-neutron
elastic-recoil field, and the Ge-intrinsic cosmogenic decays of $^{3}$H,
$^{68}$Ge, and $^{65}$Zn---we placed every channel on a single unified phonon
energy scale with no ionization quenching, and folded them through a common
bandwidth-limited response matrix $R(E_{\rm rec}\,|\,E_{\rm dep})$ for two
quasiparticle-trapping designs, Ta$\to$Al and Al$\to$Hf. The reconstructed
energy is calibrated to estimate the deposit, so the output $dR/dE_{\rm rec}$
is both what a $25$~kHz readout records and the physical energy an experiment
would report.

Four findings organize the result. The reactor-CEvNS signal reconstructs to
tens of eV, with a median near $55$~eV in $E_{\rm rec}$ and about two-thirds of
its counts below $100$~eV---but it does not occupy that band alone. The ambient
neutron-elastic recoils share its kinematics and, even under an assumed shield
of NUCLEUS-demonstrated performance, are the dominant background in the
$10$--$100$~eV region of interest at $\sim$99\% of the residual budget, giving
an in-band signal-to-background ratio of order a few, $S/B\simeq2.6$ for both
designs. This is the load-bearing conclusion: the QPD reactor-CEvNS search is
neutron-limited, not readout-limited---extending to a bandwidth-limited QPD
readout the neutron-limited character already established for reactor
CEvNS~\cite{biffl2023,conusneutron2019,ricochetneutron2023,nucleusbkg2026}. Cosmic muons, depositing
$\sim$$1.5$--$197$~MeV, drive the readout deep into saturation and collapse onto
a single tens-of-keV pile-up ($\approx$$37.6$~keV for Ta$\to$Al,
$\approx$$29.9$~keV for Al$\to$Hf)---an instrumental artifact of the modeled
ceiling, not a physical line---well clear of the signal band, with a pileup
occupancy of only $R\tau\approx3.3\times10^{-5}$ so that quiescent
reconstruction is not precluded. Beneath the shielded ambient channels, the
Ge-intrinsic cosmogenic decays---which no external shield reaches---set the
irreducible floor. Every channel is anchored: the deposited CEvNS rate
reproduces the Billard germanium benchmark to within $2.4\%$~\cite{billard2017},
the muon flux matches the Particle Data Group sea-level value within its
$\sim$$30\%$ spread, the Compton edges are fixed exactly by scattering
kinematics, the neutron flux is normalized to the Gordon sea-level
measurement~\cite{gordon2004}, and the cosmogenic activities to the SuperCDMS
and EDELWEISS germanium-activation measurements~\cite{amman2019,edelweiss2017}.

We state the scope plainly. This is a stage-one forward-model feasibility
study. It establishes the reconstructed-energy regime in which a QPD germanium
wafer would operate; it does \emph{not} compute a sensitivity and makes no claim
of detectability or discovery significance. Five modeling uncertainties bound
the interpretation. The one that governs the headline $S/B$ is the ambient
neutron rate---order-of-magnitude by label, with an \emph{assumed} rather than
computed shield and reactor-correlated neutrons omitted---so $S/B\simeq2.6$
should be read as ``of order a few.'' The remaining four act away from the
signal-to-background result: the unbenchmarked saturated-regime response shape
and the crossover deposit energy (a $\sim$$50$~eV--$13$~keV band) touch only the
muon and gamma backgrounds, while the factor-of-$\sim$2 site-dependent gamma
normalization and the cited-but-not-reproduced sub-$1.8$~MeV reactor flux
rescale rates without moving the signal off its tens-of-eV placement.

Each caveat maps to a concrete next step. An in-situ neutron survey, an
as-built shield simulation, and a reactor-neutron transport calculation would
replace the assumed suppression and are the first to retire; a direct QPD
saturation calibration would replace the modeled ceiling shape; an on-site
gamma survey would fix the absolute Compton normalization; a thin-wafer
sensor-sharing measurement would turn the crossover band into a line; and a
digitized low-energy reactor flux would collapse the signal-side band. Combined
with an exposure model and a noise-and-resolution treatment of the
reconstruction floor, these define the path from the present feasibility map to
a quantitative QPD reactor-CEvNS sensitivity---one that, on the evidence here,
will be set by the neutron background above all---extending the phonon-scale
reach of the QPD concept~\cite{ramanathan2026} into the reactor-antineutrino
program.
