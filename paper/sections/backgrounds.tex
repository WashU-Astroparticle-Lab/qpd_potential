A surface germanium wafer sits in several ambient radiation fields. Two
are electron-recoil continua from external radiation---through-going
cosmic-ray muons and the radiogenic environmental-gamma continuum---and a
third is the nuclear-recoil field of ambient fast neutrons, which
elastically scatter off germanium and are the background most dangerous to
a CEvNS search because they mimic its very signature. A fourth field is
internal: cosmogenic activation during surface exposure seeds the crystal
with the radioisotopes $^{3}$H, $^{68}$Ge, and $^{65}$Zn, whose decays
deposit energy from \emph{inside} the target. We compute each deposited
spectrum $dR/dE_{\rm dep}$ on the unified phonon scale (no ionization
quenching), and we anchor each to a published benchmark or evaluated data
library so that the downstream reconstructed spectra rest on validated
inputs rather than assumed shapes.

The four fields differ in how they respond to shielding, and this
organizes the scenario we present. The external muon, gamma, and neutron
fields can be attenuated by a passive shield and active veto; we adopt the
performance demonstrated by NUCLEUS~\cite{nucleus} as a concrete assumed
shield, with suppression factors of $1000$ for muons, $200$ for neutrons,
and $500$ for environmental gammas, and a boron-loaded moderator that also
removes the ambient thermal-neutron flux and hence the in-situ $(n,\gamma)$ capture
and $^{71}$Ge channels it would otherwise drive. The internal cosmogenic
decays, by contrast, occur within the crystal and \emph{no external shield
reaches them}: they are the irreducible floor. The reconstructed
background budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} is therefore the shielded
ambient channels stacked on the unsuppressed Ge-intrinsic floor. We
present the per-channel forward model at its unshielded rate below and
apply the assumed suppression only at the point of assembly, so that the
suppression factors remain explicit, separable modeling inputs rather than
buried assumptions.

Two further channels we examined are removed by this scenario and are
therefore not carried into the budget. The first is the thermal-neutron
family: radiative capture $(n,\gamma)$ on germanium and the in-situ
production of $^{71}$Ge (by $^{70}$Ge$(n,\gamma)$, decaying by electron
capture to the same gallium-daughter X-ray lines as the cosmogenic
$^{68}$Ge below). Both are driven by the \emph{ambient thermal} neutron
flux, which the boron-loaded moderator of the assumed shield absorbs
directly through $^{10}$B$(n,\alpha)$; with the thermal flux gone, both
channels vanish, and the identical-energy line they would have contributed
survives only through the cosmogenic $^{68}$Ge, which no shield removes.
The second is airborne $^{222}$Rn in the detector cavity, a standard
low-background concern controlled by sealing and purging the inner volume
(radon-scrubbed or boil-off nitrogen), as in operating germanium
experiments~\cite{conus2025}; we treat it as an engineering-controlled
term and do not model it. Neither omission is a physics approximation---
each is a stated consequence of the shielded, sealed configuration.

\subsection{Cosmic muons}

We sample the sea-level muon intensity from the modified-Gaisser
(``Gaisser--Guan'') parametrization~\cite{gaisser,guan2015}, which
incorporates the low-energy/large-angle correction that plain Gaisser
lacks,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dI}{dE_\mu\,d\Omega}
&= 0.14\,\Big(\frac{E_\mu}{\rm GeV}\Big)^{-2.7}\\
&\quad\times\bigg[\frac{1}{1+\tfrac{1.1 E_\mu\cos\theta^\ast}{115~{\rm GeV}}}
+\frac{0.054}{1+\tfrac{1.1 E_\mu\cos\theta^\ast}{850~{\rm GeV}}}\bigg],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gaisser}
\end{equation}
in cm$^{-2}$s$^{-1}$sr$^{-1}$GeV$^{-1}$, where $E_\mu$ is the muon
energy and the effective zenith angle
$\cos\theta^\ast=[(\cos^2\theta+P_1^2+P_2\cos^{P_3}\theta+P_4\cos^{P_5}\theta)/(1+P_1^2+P_2+P_4)]^{1/2}$
softens the horizontal divergence of the plain $\sec\theta$ scaling
($P_1$--$P_5=0.102573,\,-0.068287,\,0.958633,\,0.0407253,\,0.817285$).

Each muon piercing the wafer traverses a chord $\ell$ fixed by the
ray--box intersection of its straight trajectory with the
$10.16\times10.16\times0.20$ cm slab. The chord ranges from the $0.20$ cm
vertical thickness to the $14.37$ cm space diagonal, with the
isotropic-flux mean pinned by Cauchy's theorem to
$\langle\ell\rangle=4V/S=0.385$ cm. The deposited spectrum is the
flux-weighted, projected-area chord distribution folded with the
straggling density,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dR}{dE_{\rm dep}}
&= \frac{1}{m}\int d\Omega\,(\hat{n}\!\cdot\!\hat{\Omega})_{+}\,A
\int dE_\mu\,\frac{dI}{dE_\mu\,d\Omega}\\
&\quad\times
f_{\rm L}\!\big(E_{\rm dep};\,\Delta_p(\ell,E_\mu),\,\xi(\ell)\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:muonfold}
\end{equation}
where $m$ is the wafer mass, $A$ the entry-face area,
$(\hat{n}\!\cdot\!\hat{\Omega})_{+}$ the inward surface projection, and
$f_{\rm L}$ the Landau--Vavilov density whose mode sits at the
\emph{most-probable value} (MPV) $\Delta_p$. Using the mean deposit here,
or the Moyal approximation, would misplace the peak and suppress the
high-energy tail; we use neither. With mass thickness $x=\rho\ell$, the
width and MPV follow the standard forms
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\xi&=\frac{K}{2}\frac{Z}{A}\frac{x}{\beta^2},\\
\Delta_p&=\xi\!\left[\ln\frac{2m_ec^2\beta^2\gamma^2}{I}+\ln\frac{\xi}{I}
+j-\beta^2-\delta(\beta\gamma)\right],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:landau}
\end{equation}
with $K=0.307$ MeV\,mol$^{-1}$cm$^2$, $Z/A=0.4406$ for Ge, mean
excitation $I\approx350$ eV, $j=0.200$, and $\delta(\beta\gamma)$ the
density-effect correction. For a vertically incident minimum-ionizing
muon ($x=1.065$ g\,cm$^{-2}$) we obtain $\xi=0.072$ MeV, matching the PDG
value~\cite{pdg}, and $\Delta_p=1.23$ MeV --- strictly below the mean
deposit $\langle\Delta\rangle=1.46$ MeV. This ordering
$\Delta_p<\langle\Delta\rangle$ is the signature of the strongly
right-skewed Landau distribution and is the single most consequential
modeling choice in this channel.

Rare near-horizontal trajectories traverse up to the full $14.37$ cm
diagonal and deposit up to $\sim$197 MeV; we importance-sample these long
chords so this high-deposit tail is statistically resolved. It is the
tail --- not the $\sim$MeV bulk --- that drives the bandwidth-limited
readout into saturation, as analyzed in Sec.~\ref{sec:response}.
Integrating Eq.~\eqref{eq:muonfold} over flux and projected area gives a
total muon rate through the wafer of $1.366$ Hz. This is consistent with
the PDG sea-level expectation ($\sim$1 muon\,cm$^{-2}$min$^{-1}$,
$I_v\approx70$ m$^{-2}$s$^{-1}$sr$^{-1}$) to within $\sim$20\%, well
inside the VALD-02 validation tolerance of 30\%; the residual reflects
the known $30$--$35\%$ inter-experiment normalization spread of the
Gaisser--Guan flux and is the weakest anchor of this otherwise
textbook-grade channel.

\subsection{Environmental gammas}

The wafer sits in a radiogenic gamma field sourced by $^{40}$K and the
$^{238}$U and $^{232}$Th decay chains. The line energies are fixed
nuclear data; we take the absolute per-line fluxes from the LABChico
measured survey~\cite{labchico2022} (for example, the $^{40}$K
$1461$ keV line at $0.036$ cm$^{-2}$s$^{-1}$), with the line list
following the standard low-background compilation~\cite{heusser1995}.
Because the $2$ mm wafer is optically thin to MeV photons --- the
interaction probability is $\mu\bar\ell\approx0.117$ per mean chord at
$1$ MeV, so the double-scatter fraction is only $\sim$1.4\% --- each
photon Compton-scatters at most once and the scattered photon escapes.
The deposit is therefore the recoil-electron kinetic energy
$T_e=E_\gamma-E'$, a \emph{continuum}, and not a full-energy photopeak.

We sample the scattering angle $\theta$ from the bound-electron
incoherent cross section,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}
&=\frac{r_e^2}{2}\Big(\frac{E'}{E_\gamma}\Big)^{2}
\Big(\frac{E'}{E_\gamma}+\frac{E_\gamma}{E'}-\sin^2\theta\Big)\,S(x,Z),\\
E'&=\frac{E_\gamma}{1+\alpha(1-\cos\theta)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:kn}
\end{equation}
with classical electron radius $r_e$, incident photon energy $E_\gamma$,
scattered energy $E'$, and $\alpha=E_\gamma/m_ec^2$. The Klein--Nishina
factor~\cite{kleinnishina1929} sets the continuum shape and the
free-electron edge; the Hubbell incoherent scattering
function~\cite{hubbell1975} $S(x,Z\!=\!32)$, evaluated at the
momentum-transfer variable
\begin{equation}
x=\frac{E_\gamma\,[{\rm keV}]\,\sin(\theta/2)}{12.39842}~\text{\AA}^{-1},
\label{eq:momtransfer}
\end{equation}
imposes electron binding: since $S(x\!\to\!0)\to0$, it suppresses
near-forward (low-recoil) scattering, rolling the deposited continuum off
near $30$--$50$ eV and removing the unphysical free-Klein--Nishina
low-energy plateau, while $S(x\!\to\!\infty)\to Z$ leaves the bulk
Compton continuum unchanged. The recoil deposit for each line terminates
at the Compton edge,
\begin{equation}
E_{\rm edge}=\frac{2E_\gamma^2}{m_ec^2+2E_\gamma},
\label{eq:comptonedge}
\end{equation}
which places the $^{40}$K, $^{214}$Bi, and $^{208}$Tl edges at $1243$,
$1541$, and $2382$ keV respectively. These edge positions are the
sharpest, most model-independent features of the channel: they are fixed
by Compton kinematics alone and are reproduced by the angle-sampled
maxima to better than $0.5$ keV.

Summed over lines, the single-scatter interaction rate is $0.267$ Hz,
within $2\%$ of the independent $\Phi\,\sigma_{\rm KN}\,N_e$ estimate
($0.273$ Hz, ratio $0.98$), where $\Phi$ is the integral gamma flux,
$\sigma_{\rm KN}$ the Klein--Nishina cross section, and $N_e$ the wafer
electron number. We are explicit about the split in confidence: the edge
positions are exact (HIGH confidence), whereas the absolute
normalization inherits the site-dependent gamma flux and is a
representative estimate good to a factor of $\sim$2 (MEDIUM confidence).
The $^{238}$U-chain flux in particular is set by an assumed chain balance
rather than a directly measured line intensity, and a different
deployment site would rescale the overall rate --- but not the edge
structure --- accordingly.

\subsection{Ambient neutron elastic recoils}
\label{sec:neutrons}

Fast neutrons are the background most dangerous to a CEvNS search: they
scatter elastically off germanium nuclei and produce exactly the low-energy
nuclear recoils the signal consists of, on the same phonon scale and with no
discriminating handle. We take the sea-level ambient neutron field from the
PARMA/EXPACS analytical model~\cite{sato2015}, evaluated at a representative
mid-latitude, sea-level, ground-level point, and normalize it to the
$>10$~MeV integral flux measured by Gordon~\emph{et~al.}~\cite{gordon2004},
$\Phi(>10~{\rm MeV})=3.55\times10^{-3}$~cm$^{-2}$s$^{-1}$; the un-normalized
PARMA prediction already agrees with that anchor to $\sim$9\%. Only this
single integral is externally validated, and we attach an
order-of-magnitude accuracy label to the entire channel accordingly---the
eV--keV differential shape that actually sets the in-band recoil rate is not
independently benchmarked, and the local low-energy flux is moreover
sensitive to the moderating and reflecting material around the detector at
the tens-of-percent level.

The deposited recoil spectrum is the elastic fold
\begin{equation}
\frac{dR}{dT}=N_{\rm Ge}\!\int_{E_{\min}(T)}^{E_{\rm top}}
\phi(E_n)\,\frac{\sigma_{\rm el}(E_n)}{f\,E_n}\,dE_n,
\qquad E_{\min}=\frac{T}{f},
\label{eq:neutronfold}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{\rm el}$ the natural-germanium elastic cross section from
ENDF/B-VIII.0~\cite{endfb8}, $N_{\rm Ge}$ the target density, and
$f=\langle 4A/(1+A)^2\rangle=0.0536$ the abundance-weighted kinematic
factor relating a recoil $T$ to the incident neutron energy. Because the
epithermal flux is nearly $\phi\propto1/E_n$, the integrand behaves as
$1/E_n^2$ and is peaked at its lower limit $E_{\min}(T)$, so the recoil rate
in the $10$--$100$~eV region of interest is set by the neutron flux at
$E_{\min}=T/f\approx0.19$--$1.9$~keV---the epithermal band, precisely the
part of the spectrum most sensitive to the local environment.

A sharp step in the deposited spectrum near $5.5$~eV deserves a word of
explanation, because it is a purely kinematic feature and a useful check on
the fold. In a single elastic collision a neutron of energy $E_n$ delivers a
recoil that ranges from zero (a grazing scatter) up to a head-on maximum
$T_{\max}=f\,E_n$, with $f=4A/(1+A)^2$. For a nucleus as heavy as germanium
($A\simeq72.6$) this factor is only $f\simeq0.054$: a neutron can hand over
at most a few percent of its energy per scatter, so a given recoil $T$ can
only have come from neutrons of energy $E_n\ge T/f$. Superimposed on the
smoothly falling epithermal flux, the natural-germanium elastic cross
section is dominated near $100$~eV by a single narrow resonance in $^{73}$Ge
at $E_n=102.59$~eV, where it spikes to thousands of barns against tens of
barns off-resonance. Those resonant neutrons produce a flood of recoils---but
by the kinematic ceiling, none above $T_{\max}=f\times102.59~{\rm eV}\simeq
5.5$~eV. The result is a step: below $5.5$~eV$_{\rm dep}$ the resonance
contributes its full weight, and just above it the resonance is
kinematically forbidden and the rate drops abruptly. It is the
neutron-elastic analogue of a Compton edge---the maximum energy a
fixed-energy projectile can transfer in one scatter---made visible here
because the cross section is concentrated at a single incident energy. On
the calibrated reconstructed axis the edge maps to $\sim$5.3~eV. Folded
through the
response, the \emph{unshielded} channel integrates to $4.78\times10^{3}$
(Ta$\to$Al) and $4.83\times10^{3}$ counts~kg$^{-1}$day$^{-1}$ in the
reconstructed $10$--$100$~eV RoI. This is the largest background in the
band by nearly two orders of magnitude, and it remains the dominant term
even after the assumed neutron suppression factor of $200$.

\subsection{Ge-intrinsic cosmogenic activation}
\label{sec:cosmogenic}

Cosmic-ray spallation during above-ground exposure activates the germanium
itself, seeding it with radioisotopes that then decay from \emph{inside} the
crystal. Three dominate the sub-keV budget: $^{3}$H, $^{68}$Ge (decaying
through $^{68}$Ga), and $^{65}$Zn. We take their sea-level production rates
from the SuperCDMS/CDMSlite~\cite{amman2019} and EDELWEISS-III~%
\cite{edelweiss2017} measurements and evaluate the as-deployed activity for
a fixed one-year surface-exposure scenario with no cool-down (a
continuously operating surface run, where activation keeps building), giving
$4.05$, $18.22$, and $10.98$ decays~kg$^{-1}$day$^{-1}$ for
$^{3}$H, $^{68}$Ge, and $^{65}$Zn respectively; the longer exposures allowed
by the production-rate band raise these by up to $\sim$3$\times$. Because
these deposits are born inside the target, they carry no quenching and
\emph{no external shield attenuates them}---they are the irreducible floor
of the shielded scenario.

The $^{3}$H decay is a $\beta^-$ continuum with an $18.6$~keV endpoint; we
use the allowed spectral shape with the non-relativistic Fermi function, so
its deposited spectrum is a smooth continuum from threshold to $18.6$~keV,
of which the fraction falling in the RoI is small but nonzero. The $^{68}$Ge
and $^{65}$Zn decays proceed by electron capture, depositing the full
atomic de-excitation energy (X-ray plus Auger cascade) of the daughter shell
vacancy as discrete lines: the K, L, and M shells of the $^{68}$Ga daughter
at $10.37$~keV, $1.30$~keV, and $158.7$~eV, and of the $^{65}$Cu daughter at
$8.98$~keV, $1.10$~keV, and $120$~eV. On the calibrated reconstructed axis
these lines image near their deposited energies, only mildly compressed by
saturation above the onset: the $^{68}$Ga M line at $158.7$~eV reconstructs
to $\sim$124~eV and its L line to $\sim$0.87~keV, so the sub-keV EC lines sit
in the \emph{shoulder} just above the $100$~eV RoI rather than inside it.
Only the low-energy tail of the $^{3}$H continuum and the M-shell lines
approach the band, but as an unshielded internal floor they set the ultimate
reach of the scenario once the ambient channels are suppressed.

All background spectra are computed on the shared phonon energy grid, so
they are co-added---the ambient muon, gamma, and neutron channels under the
assumed shield suppression, the Ge-intrinsic $^{3}$H/$^{68}$Ge/$^{65}$Zn
floor unsuppressed---and folded through the detector response of
Sec.~\ref{sec:response} to yield the reconstructed background budget of
Fig.~\ref{fig:spectra}. There the neutron-elastic channel dominates the
RoI, the muon high-deposit tail is the agent of readout saturation, and the
cosmogenic floor sets the residual once the shield is applied.
