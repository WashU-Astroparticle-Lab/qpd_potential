## 1. VALD-12: the analysis window was wrong in BOTH project documents

### 1.1 What CONUS+ actually published

> "The data sets of the three detectors are fitted simultaneously in an energy window **between
> 160 eV and 800 eV**." — 2501.05206v3, l.113

So the CONUS+ signal-extraction window is **160–800 eV_ee**. This appears in **neither** authoritative
project document:

| document | stated window | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `GPD/ROADMAP.md`, `GPD/REQUIREMENTS.md` (pre-registered) | 0.4–1.0 keV_ee | **wrong** |
| `GPD/literature/SUMMARY.md` | 160 eV_ee (open-ended) | **wrong** (right lower edge, no upper edge) |
| CONUS+ 2501.05206v3 l.113 | **160–800 eV_ee** | correct |

Where each wrong number came from:

- **0.4–1 keV_ee** is a real CONUS+ window, but it is the range in which they quote *background
  component* rates, not the range they fit the signal in (ll. 205, 220, 226, 231).
- **160 eV_ee** is the lowest detector threshold (l. 104: "The lowest value for the C3 detector is
  only 160 eV"; l. 72). It is the window's lower edge with the upper edge dropped.
- Separately, **158.7 ± 1.4 eV_ee** is the ⁷¹Ge EC M-shell X-ray line (arXiv:2604.25748v1). Its
  numerical near-coincidence with the 160 eV threshold is a coincidence and has caused confusion in
  `SUMMARY.md`; the two are different quantities.

### 1.2 The S/B ≈ 0.03 target is a mismatched-window ratio and must not be used as a gate

`SUMMARY.md` derives CONUS+'s S/B ≈ 0.03 from 1.21 counts/kg/d signal ÷ 40 counts/kg/d background.
Traced to the frozen source, those two numbers are integrated over **different windows**:

| quantity | value | window | provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| signal | 395/327 = **1.208** cts/kg/d | **160–800 eV_ee** | ll. 27, 113, 117 |
| background | ≈ **40–50** cts/kg/d | **400–1000 eV_ee** | ll. 205, 220, 231–233 |

The background figure is itself reconstructed from three component statements, each given only in
0.4–1 keV_ee: neutrons (21.6 ± 3.1, "approximately half"), muons (17.4 ± 0.3, "approximately a
third"), radon (1.9–2.8, "approximately 5%").

Those two windows overlap only over 400–800 eV_ee. **S/B ≈ 0.03 is therefore not a well-defined
signal-to-background ratio in any single window**, and VALD-12 should not be adjudicated against it.

What our pipeline actually computes is the **signal-side** comparison: our geometrically-rescaled

**Verdict: VALD-12 PASSES at the correct window** (0.833 at central k, inside the stated factor 2),
with 2 of 3 quenching-bracket legs passing.

### 1.4 Why this is a correction and not window-tuning — and the check that shows it

Moving a pre-registered window *after* seeing a failure is exactly what pre-registration exists to
prevent, and the new window is the more favourable one. That is precisely when to be suspicious. Two
things distinguish this from tuning:

1. The new window is read from a **frozen primary source sentence** that neither pre-registered
   document contained. It is a corrected factual input, not a re-scoped criterion.
2. **Independent corroboration the pre-registered window was wrong:** it produced a pathological
   sensitivity to the unsourced Lindhard k — the ratio spanned a factor **~97** across k ∈ [0.13,
   0.2] (5.52e−5 → 5.36e−3), against a stated tolerance of 2. At the correct window that spread
   collapses to a factor **4.4** (0.401 → 1.765). A merely-more-flattering window would not cure the
   k-sensitivity; this one does, because the pre-registered window sat mostly *above* our 3031.7 eV_nr
   kinematic endpoint, so its integral was dominated by the endpoint cliff rather than by the physics.
   Support fraction rises 0.349 → 0.707 for the same reason.

**The ROADMAP backtracking trigger fired correctly and is now discharged:** the 3.28-decade failure
was an artifact of a wrong window, not a failure of the CEvNS calculation.

## 2. NUCLEUS does not model reactor-correlated neutrons at all

### 2.1 What their background model contains

Their budget (§5, Table 5) simulates exactly **four** components (l. 707):

1. atmospheric muons, 2. atmospheric neutrons, 3. environmental gamma rays, 4. setup material
radioactivity.

Every neutron in the model is **cosmic-ray-induced**. It is normalized to the Gordon ground-level
measurement (l. 727) and attenuated by the VNS building — factor ~5 above 10 MeV, 1.75 integrated
(ll. 453, 471). Their conclusions list the open items (absolute VNS neutron normalization, remaining
material screening, sub-keV Geant4 physics-list reliability) and **reactor-correlated neutrons are
not among them**.

### 2.2 Their only statement on it is comparative, not quantitative

"Reactor correlated background" appears twice, both times about *other* experiments:

- l. 292 — named as one of the two disputes between Dresden-II and CONUS.
- l. 302 — their work "complements" CONUS/CONUS+ and RICOCHET, "whose respective experimental sites
  feature more favorable overburden conditions albeit being **further exposed to reactor correlated
  background due to their much shorter baselines**."

So the justification is **structural and unquantified**: at 72/102 m they position themselves as less
exposed than the 10–20 m experiments. No transport calculation, no bound, no number.

### 2.3 Why this gives us no cover

We sit at **25 m** — inside the CONUS/Dresden-II regime NUCLEUS explicitly excludes itself from, not
theirs. A bare-core estimate:

```
3 GW_th / 205.8 MeV per fission  = 9.10e19 fissions/s
x ~2.4 prompt neutrons/fission   = 2.18e20 n/s
/ 4*pi*(2500 cm)^2               = 2.78e12 n cm^-2 s^-1  at 25 m
```

That is **~37% of our 7.5e12 nubar cm^-2 s^-1** antineutrino flux, from particles whose interaction
cross section is ~20 orders of magnitude larger. Only the reactor's own bioshield suppresses it, and
that is site-specific, absent from this project, and not something NUCLEUS's numbers can supply.

**Consequence:** our modelled neutron background is a **floor**, and the omission is the single
largest unquantified term in the budget. It must be stated as such in the paper, not left implicit.
